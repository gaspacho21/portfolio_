## Abstract

Public health, personal/community health behaviors, health care delivery, and the scientific community have all been impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and are consequently poised to consider substantial paradigm shifts that will enhance disease prevention and public health resilience. The current analysis compares the newly developed Lifestyle Health Index (LHI) to U.S. county-level COVID-19 vaccination, infection, and mortality rates. We linked Centers of Disease Control PLACES, the U.S. Community Profile Report, and Nationhood lab databases through common zip-code identifiers to determine the association between county-level LHI scores and COVID-19 outcomes and vaccination status against the backdrop of U.S. regions with distinct cultural phenotypes. There was a statistically significant relationship between a poor LHI, lower COVID-19 vaccination rates and higher COVID-19 infection and mortality rates. There were clear differences in outcomes across the U.S. regions, suggesting distinct regional cultural characteristics may significantly influence health behaviors and outcomes. In the U.S., a syndemic comprising unhealthy lifestyle, chronic disease, and COVID-19 resulted in unnecessary hospitalizations and deaths. Politicization of the pandemic, socioeconomic inequity and regional cultural values meaningfully contributed to the uneven distribution of poor outcomes during this syndemic. Components of the syndemic were avoidable and should not be repeated.

## Introduction

The U.S. health sector has an opportunity to learn from mistakes made during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic and to identify vulnerabilities and make strategic improvements.1, 2, 3 Public health, personal/community health behaviors, health care delivery, and the scientific community have all been implicated in and dramatically impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic.

There are numerous facets to consider when unpacking the consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic in the U.S. During the pandemic, public health guidance and initiatives played a central role in formulating COVID-19 mitigation strategies, including social distancing, stay at home guidance and, when becoming available, recommendations for vaccination.4 While these U.S. mitigation strategies were nationally applicable, clear variations in adoption became apparent across geographic regions. Moreover, lifestyle behaviors such as physical activity and dietary patterns worsened during the COVID-19 pandemic.5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 The baseline for unhealthy lifestyle behaviors was alarmingly high prior to the viral pandemic and the even higher baseline created by COVID-19 may persist into the future.12 In truth, the unhealthy lifestyle pandemic we have been entrenched in since long before the COVID-19 pandemic is a primary driver of the chronic disease crisis in the U.S. The U.S. health care system historically functions in a reactionary, secondary prevention model, treating preventable conditions after they are diagnosed. It was ill-prepared for the surge in hospitalizations that resulted from COVID-19 infection.13 Subsequent data revealed that individuals with unhealthy lifestyle behaviors or chronic disease diagnoses, such as cardiovascular disease, had dramatically poorer outcomes. Unhealthy lifestyle behaviors, chronic conditions, and the risk of symptom severity and death from COVID-19 infection were clearly linked.14, 15, 16

The current analysis furthers this work by comparing the newly developed Lifestyle Health Index (LHI)17 to U.S. county-level COVID-19 vaccination, infection, and mortality rates – comparisons were made at both the national level and distinct geographical regions with differing cultural values according to the *American Nations* model.18
